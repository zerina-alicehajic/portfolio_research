<p class="dropcap">T</p>he global transition toward green technology and digital infrastructure has radically shifted the landscape of international trade policy. Critical raw materials—ranging from rare earth elements to lithium and cobalt—have evolved from simple industrial commodities into strategic levers of statecraft.

---

### **I. Strategic Dependencies & Market Concentration**

Unlike traditional fossil fuel markets, critical mineral supply chains exhibit high geographical concentration across extraction and refining nodes. 

> *"When supply chains are concentrated in singular jurisdiction nodes, interdependence ceases to be mutual and becomes weaponized."*

#### Key Structural Vulnerabilities

* **Processing Bottlenecks:** Refining capacity remains concentrated in single regional hubs.
* **Asymmetric Interdependence:** Downstream economies face high switching costs in short-to-medium timeframes.
* **Policy Remedies:** Strategic stockpiling, bilateral trade agreements, and recycling mandates.
